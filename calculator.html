Primary strength stimulus // Squat pattern dominant
Strength
▾
🧠 Coach: This is your heaviest lower day. CNS must be fresh — do not train the day before if possible. Warm up to your working weight with 3 progressively heavier sets before hitting your working sets.

Back Squat
Bar over mid-foot, chest up, drive knees out, explode out of the hole. Do NOT grind reps — if bar speed slows dramatically, stop the set.

Hold KB/DB at chest. Full depth — crease of hip below knee. Zero knee cave. Heel must stay down. This is your primary single-leg strength builder.

C1
Romanian Deadlift
Push hips back — not down. Feel a deep stretch in the hamstrings at the bottom. Keep back flat. These build the posterior chain that launches you off the floor.
 4 × 5 70–75% 2 min

Nordic Hamstring Curl
Lock feet under bar or have partner hold. Lower as slowly as possible — catch yourself if needed. This is the #1 hamstring injury prevention exercise on the planet.
 3 × 5 Bodyweight (add band wk 5+) 90 sec
E1

Full ROM — pause at the bottom stretch. Stiff ankles kill vert. These are non-negotiable at your training age.
 4 × 12 60–65% 90 sec
F1
Tibialis Raise
Stand heels on plate, raise toes as high as possible. 3×15 bodyweight. Builds anterior shin strength — directly improves ankle stiffness and vert mechanics.

02
Tuesday — Power Day 1 (Bilateral)
Explosive output // Rate of force development // Clean first
Power
▾
🧠 Coach: Power days are about INTENT, not load. Every rep is 100% effort. If you're not fully recovered mentally and physically, reduce volume — never reduce effort. Take full rest between power sets — partial recovery = partial adaptation.
#ExerciseSets × Reps% 1RM / LoadRest
A1
Power Clean
ALWAYS FIRST. Bar close to body, violent hip extension, shrug hard, pull yourself under. Triple extension — ankle, knee, hip — is the exact same pattern as jumping. This is the king of vert development.
 5 × 3 75–80% 3 min full
B1
Depth Jump
Step off box (not jump). Land both feet simultaneously. Ground contact under 0.12 seconds — think "hot coals". Immediately explode to max height. This trains your stretch-shortening cycle — the elastic component of your vert.
 5 × 5 BW — absolute max intent 2 min
C1
Trap Bar Jump Squat
Load is deliberately light — this is a speed exercise. Descend quickly, reverse immediately, jump as high as possible every rep. Never let the weight slow your bar speed.
 5 × 4 30% squat 1RM 90 sec
D1
Standing Broad Jump
Max distance — use arm swing. Stick the landing. Full reset and reset between reps. Measures horizontal power which directly correlates to vert.
 5 × 3 BW — max distance 90 sec
E1
Max Height Box Jump
Find the highest box you can hit cleanly. Step DOWN — never drop jump from a height. Quality over quantity. If you miss 2 in a row, drop box height.
 5 × 3 BW — max height 2 min
F1
Pogo Hops
Stiff ankles, minimal knee bend, maximum rebound height. Think of your legs as springs. This builds the reactive ankle stiffness elite jumpers have.
 3 × 20 BW — stiff and fast 60 sec
03
Wednesday — Max Strength Upper + Core
Upper body + core // Arm swing & stability for vert
Upper
▾
🧠 Coach: Upper body directly contributes 2–4 inches to your vert through arm swing mechanics and core transfer. Core work here is anti-rotation and anti-extension — not crunches. These patterns stabilize your landing and drive force through your whole body on takeoff.
#ExerciseSets × Reps% 1RMRest
A1
Weighted Pull-Up
Full dead hang at bottom, chin clearly over bar. Use a dip belt. Lat strength is critical for the arm-swing component of jumping — often overlooked.
 5 × 4 80–85% 3 min
B1
Incline Barbell Bench Press
45° incline. Upper chest and anterior deltoid. Builds the upper body pressing power that transfers to arm swing stability at peak jump height.
 4 × 5 80–85% 2–3 min
C1
Barbell Pendlay Row
Bar starts on floor each rep. Explosive pull to lower chest. Horizontal pull balances vertical pressing and builds the posterior chain link between upper and lower body power.
 4 × 5 75–80% 2 min
D1
Ab Wheel Rollout
From knees initially, progress to standing. Full extension if possible. Anti-extension core — this is the same pattern your core uses on jump takeoff to transfer force from legs to upper body.
 4 × 10 Bodyweight 90 sec
E1
Pallof Press (Cable)
Anti-rotation. Stand side-on to cable, press straight out — resist the pull to rotate. Builds rotational core stability that stabilizes your single-leg pistol takeoff.
 3 × 12 each Cable / moderate load 60 sec
F1
Hanging Leg Raise
Dead hang, raise legs to parallel or above. No swinging. Hip flexor and lower ab strength — these muscles fire first in the jump prep phase.
 3 × 12 Bodyweight 60 sec
04
Thursday — Power Day 2 (Unilateral)
Single-leg explosive power // Pistol-to-jump is the key drill
Power
▾
🧠 Coach: Single-leg power is what separates good jumpers from elite jumpers. Your vert on one leg should be within 3–4 inches of your two-leg vert by week 12. The Pistol-to-Jump drill is the centerpiece of this day — treat it like a max effort every single rep.
#ExerciseSets × RepsLoadRest
A1
Pistol Squat → Explosive Jump
THE KEY DRILL. Descend into full pistol — complete depth, controlled — then EXPLODE into a single-leg jump as high as possible. Trains the full strength-to-explosion chain on one leg. This is your most important single exercise for pistol-specific vert.
 5 × 4 each BW — max jump height 2 min
B1
Single-Leg Depth Jump
Step off box, land on one foot, immediately explode up on same leg. Forces your ankle and knee to absorb and redirect force unilaterally. This is advanced — start with a low box (12–18") and build up.
 4 × 4 each BW — max intent 2 min
C1
Single-Leg Broad Jump
One leg takeoff AND landing. Stick the landing — control is power. Alternate legs. Track distance each session and try to beat it.
 4 × 3 each BW — max distance 90 sec
D1
Reactive Step-Up Jump
Step up onto box with one foot, drive opposite knee up aggressively, explode as high as possible. Land both feet on ground. Mimics the run-up jump takeoff pattern.
 3 × 5 each BW — max height 90 sec
E1
Lateral Bound (Stick)
Explode laterally off one leg, land and STICK on the other. No extra hops. Hold the landing for 1 second — this builds lateral hip stability which protects knees and improves jump mechanics.
 3 × 8 each BW — controlled 60 sec

Contrast
▾
🧠 Coach: Contrast training is the most powerful tool for vert development. You do a heavy lift (which activates your fast-twitch muscle fibers and primes the CNS), then immediately follow with an explosive movement. The result: you jump higher than you would cold. Rest 45–60 seconds MAX between the heavy set and the jump — the window closes after 90 seconds. This is your most important training day.

Deload Protocol — Weeks 4, 8, 11
Cut total volume by 40%. Keep ALL intensity percentages the same — do not reduce load. Reduce sets by 40%, not weight. Example: 5×3 becomes 3×3 at the same %. Remove all plyometric volume during deload weeks. Continue mobility and recovery work. Deloads are not optional — skipping them guarantees a plateau or injury within this 12-week block.
Pistol System
// Complete weighted pistol squat progression — strength, tempo, and power phases

Why Weighted Pistols Change Your Vert

A loaded pistol squat trains single-leg force production at full joint range of motion — the deepest position your body reaches before exploding in a jump. When you can produce massive force from the bottom of a pistol, your jump takeoff becomes dramatically more powerful. Combined with the Pistol-to-Jump drill, you're training the full spectrum from maximal strength to explosive expression on one leg.

⚡ The Pistol-to-Jump Drill — Step by Step

This is your most important drill for pistol-specific explosiveness. Master this and your single-leg vert will be elite.

1
Stand on one leg. Raise opposite leg slightly off the ground. Arms at your sides, chest tall.
2
Descend into a full pistol squat — controlled. Crease of hip below knee. Heel stays flat on the floor. Opposite leg straight in front. This is the strength phase.
3
Pause 1 second at the bottom. Do not bounce. Own the position. This is what separates this drill from a regular jump squat.
4
Drive through your heel violently. Swing both arms up aggressively. EXPLODE into a single-leg jump as high as physically possible. This is the power phase.
5
Land softly on both feet or the same leg. Reset fully. Full rest between reps — 30–45 seconds. Every rep is a max effort.

Nutrition Protocol
// The program only works if fuel matches training stress — this is part of the program
0.9g
Protein per lb bodyweight / day
At 150lbs = 135g protein daily minimum. Muscle protein synthesis requires sustained amino acid availability. Chicken, beef, eggs, Greek yogurt, whey. Hit this every single day — training or not.
2–3g
Carbs per lb on training days
At 150lbs = 300–450g carbs on heavy days. Carbohydrates fuel explosive training. Low-carb and vert do not mix. Rice, oats, fruit, potatoes. Do not fear carbs around training.
0.5g
Fat per lb bodyweight / day
At 150lbs = 75g fat. Healthy fats support testosterone production and joint health. Avocado, olive oil, nuts, whole eggs. Don't go too low on fat — it crashes hormones at your age.
+300
Calorie surplus / day
You're 18–25 and under 160lbs. You need to eat above maintenance to build the muscle that drives vert gains. A 300 calorie surplus keeps you lean while allowing muscle growth. Track calories for at least the first 4 weeks.
Nutrient Timing
90 min pre
Full meal: Carbs + protein + low fat. Rice and chicken. Oats and eggs. Give your body time to digest before explosive work. Training on empty = 15–20% performance drop.
30 min pre
Optional fast carbs: Banana, rice cake, or sports drink if you need a top-up. Don't eat a meal this close — blood goes to digestion, not muscles.
During (power days)
Water + electrolytes: Even mild dehydration (2%) reduces power output measurably. On long sessions, sip a sports drink or electrolyte mix between sets.
Within 30 min post
40g protein + fast carbs immediately: Whey shake + banana or rice cakes. The post-workout window is real for glycogen replenishment. Don't skip this after strength or contrast days.
2 hrs post
Full meal: Second protein hit. Your muscles stay in a protein synthesis window for 4–6 hours after training. A second meal with protein maximizes this.
Before bed
Casein protein or cottage cheese: Slow-digesting protein feeds muscle repair during the 9 hours of sleep. This is when your body actually builds the muscle you trained for. Don't go to bed in a protein deficit.
Non-Negotiables
// Break any of these and the program stops working — no exceptions
01
Power Cleans Always Go First
On Tuesday and Saturday, the power clean is always exercise A1 — the very first thing you do. Your central nervous system needs to be completely fresh for max-intent cleans. If you do them after heavy squats you lose 15–20% of the training stimulus. Never negotiate this.
02
Contrast Rest Window is 45–60 Seconds Max
On Saturday, you have 45–60 seconds between the heavy lift and the explosive movement. Post-activation potentiation — the CNS priming effect — disappears after 90 seconds. Set a timer. This is what makes contrast training work. Miss the window and you're just doing supersets with extra steps.
03
Every Plyo Rep is 100% or Don't Do It
Power training at 80% effort builds 80% athletes. If you're fatigued and can't give max intent on your depth jumps or broad jumps, cut the set short. Do not train plyo work in a fatigued state — you engrain slow movement patterns into your nervous system. Partial effort trains partial output.
04
Deload Every 4th Week — No Exceptions
Weeks 4, 8, and 11 are deloads. Cut volume 40%, keep intensity the same. The athletes who skip deloads are the ones who plateau at week 8 wondering why they stopped making gains. Supercompensation — the process that makes you stronger — requires a recovery window. Build it in or your body will force it on you through injury or burnout.
05
Test Everything in Week 1 and Week 12
Before training starts: max vert, pistol 1RM, back squat 1RM, power clean 1RM, hip thrust 1RM, standing broad jump distance. Write it down. After week 12: retest all. You cannot know if a program is working without measuring. Athletes who skip baseline testing have no idea if they're improving or spinning wheels.
06
Sunday Recovery is Not Optional
Active recovery is training. The mobility work keeps your hips and ankles from becoming the bottleneck in your vert mechanics. The low-intensity movement flushes metabolic waste. The 9 hours of sleep triggers HGH release. At 18–25, you have the highest HGH potential of your life. A 6-hour night wastes it. Treat Sunday like Saturday.
07
Pistol Depth is Non-Negotiable
A partial-depth pistol squat trains partial-range strength. The jump takeoff starts from full knee flexion. If you're not getting full depth on your pistols, you're training a movement that doesn't transfer to what you actually want. Reduce the load until you own the depth, then add weight back. No exceptions.
08
Eat Enough to Fuel the Training
At under 160lbs and 18–25, undereating is the most common reason athletes on this type of program fail to make gains. You are doing 6 intense training days per week. Your calorie needs are high. If you're losing weight or not recovering, eat more. Vert is built on muscle — and muscle is built on food. This is not negotiable.
